# The Expedition of the Thousand (1860)
### A Historical GIS Project on Movement and Unification

## Overview
This project maps the route of the **Expedition of the Thousand**, led by Giuseppe Garibaldi in 1860, a key event in the Italian unification process.

The map reconstructs the movement from **Quarto (Genoa)** to **Naples**, highlighting the main stages of the campaign and the role of geography in its success.

---

## Project Focus
The goal of this project is to represent a **historical movement** through GIS and explore how spatial dynamics contributed to a major political transformation.

Rather than focusing only on locations, the project emphasizes:
- movement
- sequence of events
- strategic geography

---

## Data and Sources
The mapped route includes:

- Starting point: **Quarto (Genoa)**
- Key stops:
  - Talamone
  - Marsala
  - Palermo
  - Messina
  - Calabria
- Final destination: **Naples**

The data is based on historical records of the expedition, which is well documented and allows for relatively accurate geolocation. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}  

Coordinates were obtained using QGIS tools and modern geographic references.

---

## Interpretation

The map supports a key interpretation:

Garibaldi’s campaign followed a **bottom-up strategy**.

Instead of starting from the North with strong institutional support, the expedition:
- began with a small force
- landed in Sicily
- gradually moved northward
- gained control step by step

This suggests that:
- geography and mobility were central
- unification was not purely top-down
- territorial control emerged progressively through movement

---

## Mapping and Visualization

### Routes
Two types of movement are represented:

- **Sea routes** → blue dashed line  
- **Land routes** → solid red line  

This distinction makes the transition between naval and land phases immediately visible.

---

### Symbols
Custom symbols were used to improve readability:

- ⚔️ Crossed swords → battle locations  
- 🏠 House → starting point  
- 🚩 Flag → final destination  

These elements help the viewer quickly understand the nature of each location.

---

### Interactivity
The map includes an interactive feature:

- Hovering over a point shows:
  - location name
  - date
  - short description of the event

This transforms the map into an **exploratory tool**, not just a static visualization. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  

---

## Results

The map highlights:

- a clear **southward naval movement** to Sicily
- followed by a **northward land progression**
- a sequence of strategic stops and battles

The visual structure makes it easy to understand:
- the order of events
- the importance of specific locations
- the overall direction of the campaign

---

## Limitations

- The exact paths between locations, especially at sea, are approximate
- The map focuses on **key events**, not precise trajectories
- Some simplifications were necessary for clarity

However, these limitations do not affect the main goal of the project: representing the **structure and logic of the movement**.
